About this project

A prototype information tool that helps families of children with cancer understand the medical information they receive, one question at a time.

Why it exists

This project began with time spent volunteering with children receiving cancer treatment at Tata Memorial Hospital in India. Families were often given a great deal of complex information at once: new medical words, treatment plans, unfamiliar procedures and uncertainty about what comes next. Under stress, much of it was hard to take in.

The idea is simple: a calm, private place where a parent, caregiver or older child can ask a question in their own words and get a short, clear explanation, plus good questions to take back to their care team.

The concept and conversational framework were initiated by the project's Technical Lead, who also designed and built this prototype.

What it is, and what it is not

  • It is an independent prototype. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or used at Tata Memorial Centre or any other hospital or organisation.
  • It has not been clinically validated, has not been reviewed by doctors for this prototype, and has not been used with patients.
  • It gives general educational information only. It does not diagnose, interpret individual results, estimate prognosis, recommend treatment changes or give medicine doses.
  • It is meant to support conversations with the care team, never to replace them.

Where the information comes from

Content is written in plain language and summarised in our own words from public education material by the National Cancer Institute, the American Cancer Society, St. Jude Children's Research Hospital (Together by St. Jude), the World Health Organization and Tata Memorial Centre. Each answer lists the sources it draws on. Every link was opened and checked on . These organisations do not endorse this project.

Privacy

  • No login, no registration and no tracking or analytics.
  • Questions are answered inside your browser. Nothing you type is sent to a server.
  • The conversation is not saved. Starting a new conversation or closing the page clears it.
  • The appointment checklist is saved on this device only if you choose to turn that on.
  • This prototype is not a medical record system and does not claim to meet healthcare privacy standards.

How it works

A technical overview for reviewers. The family-facing conversation does not show any of this unless you turn on match details below.

Architecture

A local-first web app written in plain JavaScript with no build step and no runtime dependencies. Content, matching, safety rules and interface are separate files:

  • src/data/ knowledge base: topics with short answers, optional details, FAQs, questions for the care team, follow-ups and source ids; a glossary; a source registry; the checklist question bank.
  • src/engine/text.js normalisation, British and Indian English spellings, common misspellings, everyday phrases mapped to medical terms, light stemming, edit distance.
  • src/engine/safety.js rule-based safety classifier that runs before any retrieval.
  • src/engine/retrieval.js weighted, IDF-scored retrieval over topics and FAQs.
  • src/engine/responder.js decision order, confidence checks, response objects, plain-text copy.
  • src/ui/ rendering and interaction.

It does not use a large language model, machine learning or embeddings. Every answer is curated text selected by deterministic rules, which makes behaviour predictable and testable.

What happens to a message

1

Normalise

Lower-case, expand contractions, fix known misspellings ("biospy", "leukaemia"), and map everyday phrases to knowledge-base terms ("hair fall" to hair loss, "throwing up" to vomiting).

2

Safety check

Crisis language, emergencies and urgent symptoms described as happening now take over the reply with a short, direct message. Requests for diagnosis, interpretation of results, prognosis, treatment decisions or doses get a boundary reply that explains why and offers questions for the team.

3

Intents and definitions

Greetings, "who are you", privacy questions, "tell me more" and "open the glossary" are handled directly. "What does X mean" checks the glossary first.

4

Score every topic and FAQ

Weighted keyword overlap using IDF, cosine similarity with curated question variants, phrase bonuses and fuzzy matching for misspellings. Short follow-ups borrow the previous topic's terms.

5

Decide how confident to be

A low score, or a match only on incidental words, gives an honest "I'm not sure" with nearby topics. Two close matches give a clarifying question. Otherwise the best answer is shown, essentials first, with details folded away.

An answer needs a score of at least and at least one match on a keyword or title word. A second topic scoring within of the first triggers a clarifying question, unless the first is already a strong match.

Safety tiers

TierExampleResponse
Crisis"I want to die"Support message, 112 and Tele-MANAS 14416. No other content.
Emergency"She is not breathing"Call emergency services first line, then the team.
Urgent"My son has a fever"Cannot assess; contact the treating team now; emergency route. No long explanation underneath.
Boundary"Will my child survive?"Explains the limit, gives general context and questions to ask the team.
Banner"Why is fever dangerous during chemo?"Normal educational answer with a short urgent-help note on top.

Symptom words in a general question do not trigger a takeover, and past events ("had a fever last week") add a banner instead. The rules are cautious but cannot catch everything, which is why the interface always points to the treating team.

Testing

An automated suite (node tests/engine.test.js) checks the required example questions, synonyms and misspellings, unknown and ambiguous questions, urgent and emergency reports, crisis language, unsafe personal requests, privacy notices, follow-up context, that every suggested follow-up leads to a real answer, that every source is linked and verified, and that content contains no doses or fever thresholds.
